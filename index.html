How we estimate an athlete's value

Every valuation models 12-month NIL earning potential from four inputs: audience (social reach and engagement), on-field role and performance, program and market size, and the sport and position. Where a figure has been publicly reported we anchor to it and say so on the athlete's page; everywhere else the number is a modeled estimate shown as a range, not a promise. Data comes from official team rosters, verified public social accounts and public reporting, and every page shows when it was last updated. Read the full methodology.

Common questions

Whose NIL value can I look up?

Search our database for top college athletes, and we add more after every research run. If a player isn't in yet, use Estimate any athlete to model their value from their following and profile.

What is NIL?

NIL means Name, Image & Likeness. Since 2021, college athletes (and high schoolers in many states) can get paid for ads, social posts, autographs and more. They don't lose their spot on the team.

How accurate are these numbers?

They're smart estimates, not confirmed deals, which is why we show a range. We use the same factors brands look at and sense-check against public figures (like On3) where they exist. Real deals can be higher or lower.

Why do likes matter more than followers?

Brands pay for fans who pay attention. A small page where lots of people like and comment can be worth more than a big page where no one does.

Can high school athletes earn NIL money?

Yes. Many states let high schoolers do NIL deals. Local shops, camps and gear brands are common partners.

Do you save my info?

The details you type stay on your device. We only receive your email when you submit it to unlock a value. See our Privacy Policy.
